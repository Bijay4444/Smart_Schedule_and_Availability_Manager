Users can now create a weekly commitment, change its hours from a future date, or move or cancel just one occurrence. In the demo, Job A changes from nine to one, to ten to two, starting November thirtieth. Earlier weeks retain their original hours. Before a recurring change is saved, the app shows a dated review and flags conflicts. Overnight commitments and daylight-saving choices are handled in the scheduling services, rather than scattered through views.

Smart Schedule / Final seminar04 / 10
Availability was still a planned calculation in my previous talk. Users now set working windows, including date-specific overrides, and the app subtracts one-time and recurring commitments. The same Monday example now works: a working window from eight to eight, a class from ten to twelve, and study from twelve to two leave eight to ten and two to eight. The employer-facing text contains only dates and available hours, so private reasons for being busy are not shared. Users can copy this result directly.

Smart Schedule / Final seminar05 / 10
The shift checker answers: can I accept this particular offer? It checks the whole interval against commitments and working windows. A Monday offer from eleven to eleven-thirty conflicts with the class. Hours outside a working window are reported separately. Checking does not save anything. If a clock change makes a date uncertain, the app asks for review instead of confidently reporting that time as available.

Smart Schedule / Final seminar07 / 10
The app now has separate development and production settings and is packaged with Docker. On one Azure Ubuntu virtual machine, Caddy handles HTTPS, Gunicorn serves Django, and PostgreSQL keeps schedules in persistent storage. My custom domain points to that server. This simple architecture fits the university demonstration and student credit. Backups are supported, but the app and database share a server; I am not claiming high availability.

Smart Schedule / Final seminar08 / 10
GitHub Actions now tests pull requests and automatically deploys successful pushes to the main branch. The pipeline builds and rehearses a Docker image, then deploys that exact tested image. Before migrations, it takes a database backup. It verifies the app and public HTTPS address before reporting success. Azure access uses OpenID Connect, and the runner's temporary SSH firewall rule is removed afterward. The first complete automated deployment has already passed.
